Work in Progress

  • Working Conditions and Health at Work Over the Life Cycle: Evidence from Europe
    With Michele Belloni and Claudio Lucifora
    The study examines how cumulative exposure to adverse working conditions affects later-life physical and mental health among Europeans. Using longitudinal data from SHARE linked to historical occupational measures from O*NET and the 1965 DOT, it applies an instrumental-variable approach based on Bartik-type variation to address endogeneity and measurement error. Health is modeled as a durable capital stock that depreciates with age and harmful work environments. The analysis constructs long-term indices of physical and psychosocial strain and reveals gender-differentiated effects, emphasizing the lasting impact of working conditions on health and the importance of improving job quality for an aging workforce.
  • Does It Take a Village to Raise a Child? Parental Health Shocks and Fertility Timing in the Netherlands
    With Viola Angelini and Max Groneck
    The project examines how unexpected health shocks within the family influence women's fertility timing and the mechanisms through which these effects operate inside the household. Using linked Dutch administrative data combining hospital records, employment histories, and demographic information, the analysis follows families over time and studies short- and medium-term responses to parental health shocks. An event-study design compares women whose parents experience a sudden health shock with similar women whose parents do not, allowing fertility trajectories to be tracked before and after the event. The framework highlights how caregiving responsibilities, financial pressures, and the potential loss of future childcare support may delay childbearing, particularly when shocks occur during critical reproductive ages. The project also investigates spillover effects of grandparental death, examining whether the loss of informal childcare provided by grandparents leads families to postpone or adjust fertility decisions.
